The Well-Equipped Chamundeshwari Studio, Run by a Strict Owner

A cameraman once smoked a cigarette on the studio premises and threw the cigarette butt on the ground. “Hey, Mister, pick that up,” said the owner, Ramulu Naidu.
— A memory shared by still photographer Pragathi Ashwathanarayana about Chamundeshwari Studio.

-------------------------------------

Among the film studios in Karnataka, Bengaluru’s Chamundeshwari Studio holds a special place. The studio was renowned for its discipline, cleanliness, and an environment conducive to filmmaking.

Its first owner, Sri Ramulu Naidu, had been running Pakshiraja Studio in Coimbatore. Witnessing the growth of the Karnataka film industry in the 1970s, he renamed his studio Chamundeshwari Studio and shifted it to Bengaluru.

Initially, the studio had one floor, which was later expanded to three floors. It had all the facilities required for filmmaking, including a laboratory, song recording, editing, and dubbing facilities. Another advantage was its location in the heart of the city.

The studio was known for a level of discipline that was unmatched by other studios. Nobody was allowed to smoke on the shooting sets. In later years, an exception was made, but smokers were required to use ashtrays.

Once, a cameraman smoked a cigarette on the studio premises and threw the cigarette butt on the ground as he walked away. He did not notice that the owner, Sri Ramulu Naidu, was standing behind him. Angered by this, the owner said, “Hey, Mister, pick that up.”

Knowing the strict rules of the studio, the cameraman said nothing. He picked up the cigarette butt and put it in a nearby dustbin. There was another cigarette butt lying next to it. The owner then said, “Pick that one up too. You might have thrown that one as well.”

Without saying a word, the cameraman picked up the other cigarette butt, put it in the dustbin, and walked away.

This incident happened right before my eyes. It was a lesson in discipline—and a punishment, both at once!

At present, the studio no longer has shooting floors. Only a few departments are still functioning.
