How Was Sync Sound Recording Done on Film Sets?

Until around 1971–72, films commonly used sync sound recording, where the actors’ voices were recorded live during filming. It was only after this period that voice dubbing became widely established.

Back then, actors had to master not only their performances but also the modulation and variations in their voices. Since film stock was expensive, filmmakers could not afford to shoot too many takes.

The photograph here offers a glimpse of how sync sound recording was done. This was during the outdoor shooting of Ramanathan’s Hridaya Sangama, featuring Rajkumar and Bharathi.

A microphone was attached to a long pole-like device known as a boom, positioned above the actors. The actors had to carefully modulate their voices while also keeping the distance from the microphone in mind. It was a challenging task!

Films of that era were made within many such limitations and constraints.
