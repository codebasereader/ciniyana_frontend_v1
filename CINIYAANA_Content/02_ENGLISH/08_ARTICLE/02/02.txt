Madakari Nadu and Cinema

Puttanna’s Nagarahavu and G.V. Iyer’s Hamsageethe enhanced the appeal of Chitradurga Fort. Since then, several films have been shot at the fort, giving further impetus to cinematic activities in the region.

Shashidhar Chitradurga, Journalist

“Haavina Dvesha Hanneradu Varusha…”
“Kannada Naadina Veera Ramanıya, Gandu Bhoomiya Veera Naariya…”

Even today, whenever we hear these songs from Puttanna Kanagal’s Nagarahavu, the magnificent Chitradurga Fort instantly comes alive before our eyes.

The characters created with authentic native flavour by writer Ta.Ra.Su. carry one kind of weight, while Puttanna’s direction, which brought those characters to life and captured the fort so effectively, carries another. In the film, Puttanna transformed the fort itself into a character.

Supported by music, lyrics, and cinematography that complemented his vision, Nagarahavu (1972) became a true “film of the fort.” The movie undoubtedly made Chitradurga Fort and other tourist attractions in the region even more popular. It has now been nearly half a century since the film was released, yet Kannadigas visiting the fort still identify the places where the character Ramachari once walked!

Ta.Ra.Su.’s novel Hamsageethe was filmed in Chitradurga under the direction of G.V. Iyer and released in 1975. This music-oriented film gave the fort a unique cultural dimension. Free from the conventional framework of commercial cinema, Hamsageethe illuminated the fort in its own distinctive way.

After the film, the rock behind Gopal Swamy Honda inside the fort came to be popularly known as “Hamsageethe Kallu” (Hamsageethe Rock).

These two films not only attracted tourists to Chitradurga but also became turning points in the professional lives of the actors who appeared in them. They strengthened the local passion for cinema and inspired people from the region to become involved in the film industry. Since then, several people from the district have been active in acting, direction, film production, investment, and distribution.

Another proud literary figure from Chitradurga, B.L. Venu, is also closely associated with the fort. Veteran filmmaker T.S. Nagabharana adapted Venu’s work Kallarali Hoovagi into a film of the same name in 2006. Set against a historical backdrop, this significant experiment combined a fictional love story with a message of communal harmony.

A major portion of the film was shot at the fort itself. It was particularly significant that Ambareesh, who had made his silver-screen debut as Jaleela in Puttanna’s Nagarahavu, portrayed the role of Madakari Nayaka in Kallarali Hoovagi. The film received the National Award in the National Integration category.

Another major film based on the works of B.L. Venu was Bichhugatti, centred on the Nayakas of Chitradurga. The film arrived on screen with a high budget and extensive use of graphic technology, but ironically suffered a setback due to the COVID-19 pandemic.

Meanwhile, the film on the legendary warrior woman Onake Obavva was also released. Both films were based on Venu’s literary work. He is now writing the dialogues for the Darshan-starrer historical film Gandugali Madakari Nayaka.

The star-led historical film is being made on a large budget, with veteran filmmaker S.V. Rajendra Singh Babu at the helm. The people of Chitradurga hope that the history of the Nayakas will be brought to the screen effectively through spectacular visuals and Venu’s powerful dialogues.

The famous Kannada filmmaking duo Dora–Bhagavan adapted several works of Ta.Ra.Su. for the screen. Chitradurga and its surroundings often appear in the characters, situations, and locations created by Ta.Ra.Su. The directors retained these references in their films and, as far as possible, remained faithful to the native flavour of the stories.

G.V. Iyer, who directed Hamsageethe at the fort, returned to Chitradurga for another shoot in the late 1990s. His Kannada-Hindi television series Rani Shantala was filmed at the fort and Chandravalli.

Over the past two decades, songs and individual scenes from several films have been filmed at the fort and other tourist attractions in Chitradurga. Some notable films include Shrigandha, Hudugaru, Adrushta, Banta, Dodmane Hudga, Ajay, Rose, Jyothiraj, Sujidaara, and Padde Huli.

Saikumar’s Durgada Huli attracted attention for its distinctive story. The climax of the hugely successful Mr. and Mrs. Ramachari was filmed at Tuppada Kola. The film’s hero, Yash, played a fan of actor Vishnuvardhan. The director’s idea of evoking Vishnuvardhan’s iconic Ramachari character worked remarkably well.

Director Suri also captured Chitradurga beautifully in his film Kendasampige, earning immense appreciation from cinema lovers in the region.

Today, enthusiastic young filmmakers are attempting to capture the beauty of Chitradurga in album songs as well. KA 16, directed by Vinayak, is one successful example.

In recent years, Chitradurga has developed a special connection with the film fraternity. Audio launches and film promotional events are frequently held at the fort.

“The relationship between central Karnataka’s Chitradurga and the film industry goes back a long way. People from this region have been investing in films since the 1950s. Apart from business and film distribution, a large number of artistes and technicians from here have entered the film industry. The works of the great writer Ta.Ra.Su. brought Chitradurga alive on the silver screen. That path became an inspiration for us as well,” says writer B.L. Venu.

Venu is among the few writers in Karnataka who have served as a bridge between literature and cinema. He understands the limitations involved when a story or novel is adapted into a film.

“Most people feel that the satisfaction they get from reading a novel cannot be replicated by watching a film based on that story. Cinema demands an entirely different canvas, so changes are inevitable. A film can be based on a novel, but it is difficult to simply turn the novel itself into a film. Despite all these limitations, our writers have created many stories in which Chitradurga itself can become a character,” says B.L. Venu.
